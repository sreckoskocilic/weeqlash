How to play

Multiplayer board game. Pick a board size (4×4 up to 10×10), deploy pegs, move them across tiles. Every tile has a trivia category — land on it, answer a question. Get it right and you hold the tile. Get it wrong and you wasted a move.

Each turn you get 3 moves. Spend them however you want — advance, flank, pick fights. Walk a peg onto an enemy-occupied tile and combat starts: up to 3 questions, each correct answer deals 1 HP to the defender. Miss once and the fight is over. Pegs have 3 HP and never heal.

On boards 5×5 and larger, each player's starting corner is their flag. Reach an opponent's flag and answer 3 questions correctly to capture it and win instantly.

Qlashique

1v1 live

1v1 trivia duel · attack, heal, KO at 0 HP

How to play

1v1 trivia duel. Both players start with 15 HP (configurable: 10/15/20/30) and take turns. One player clicks CREATE GAME and shares the 5-letter code. The other types the code and clicks JOIN.

When it's your turn, click ENGAGE to start the timer. You get 10 s to click it, then it starts on its own. Questions appear one by one — correct: +1, wrong: −1. Keep answering until the timer runs out or you click END TURN. Timer starts at 5 s and grows +3 s each round (max 25 s).

Don't go AFK. A turn with no answer and no END TURN costs you 2 HP. Do it twice in a row and you lose.

Your final score determines what happens:

  • Negative — you deal that much damage to yourself.
  • 0 — nothing happens.
  • 1 — attacks the opponent for 1 HP.
  • 2 or more — choose: attack for full score damage, or heal +2 HP.

The HEAL button appears during your turn (disabled if score < 2). Clicking it heals +2 HP and ends your turn.

Qlashword

1v1 live

1v1 Scrabble · unlock 2×/3× squares with trivia

How to play

1v1 Scrabble with a trivia catch. One player makes a room and shares the 5-letter code; the other joins and the host starts. Normal 15×15 board, 100 tiles, standard letter values.

Drag or tap tiles from your rack to spell a word. Your first word has to cross the centre. Everything has to be a real word, crossing words included. Hit SUBMIT when you're done.

The catch: the 2×/3× letter and word squares are locked. For every one your new tiles cover, you get a trivia question. Answer right and the multiplier counts this turn. Miss it or run out of time and you keep the base points — no bonus.

You get 90 seconds a turn. Stuck? PASS, or SWAP tiles back into the bag (you lose the turn). SHUFFLE just reorders your rack. Play all 7 tiles at once for a +50 bingo. The game ends when the bag's empty and someone empties their rack, or when both players pass twice in a row. Highest score wins.

HowHigh?

async 1v1

Async 2P · 4 bonus types · challenge a friend

How to play

Solo trivia challenge you can send to a friend. Answer 10 questions (13 s each). Correct: +2, wrong or timeout: −2.

After question 3 the game offers one of two bonuses. Dice: you see the roll first, then decide. Question 4 pays 2 + the dice sum if correct, and costs 2 + half the sum (rounded up) if wrong or timed out. Double or Nothing: questions 4 and 5 pay double (+4/−4). You can decline either one.

After question 6, another bonus. Go Wild: 2 extra questions added (12 total) but timer drops to 10 s for the rest. Time Crunch: questions 7 and 8 get a 7-second timer with +3/−3 stakes. Again, you can decline.

When finished you get a 5-letter code. Share it with a friend — they play the same questions with the same dice roll. Highest score wins; ties broken by speed.

How to play

Solo gauntlet. 20 questions, 12 seconds each. No opponents, no pauses between questions. Correct: +13, wrong: −7, skip or timeout: 0. Perfect run is +260. Skipping is free — use it when you don't know.

How to play

Solo. Ten problems, 45 seconds each, and you type the answer instead of picking from a list. Calculus, geometry, trig, limits, the odd graph to read off. None of it comes from a question bank — every problem is generated fresh for the run, so memorising won't save you.

You don't have to nail it exactly. Land inside the tolerance and you get full marks; drift further out and the points fall off bit by bit; miss by a mile and it's zero. A few problems hand out a small bonus when you get really close. Each question is worth 10 to start.

The answer never shows up — not mid-round, not on the review screen at the end. All you get back is whether each one was right, partial, or wrong, and what it earned you.

How to play

Solo geography. We show you a country outline — just the shape, no name on it — and a grid of 60 things: cities, rivers, mountains, clubs, famous people, border claims, a few bare numbers. Some belong to that country, most don't. Tick every one that fits.

You're never told how many are right. Could be eight, could be twenty-five, and it shifts every run, so there's no pattern to lean on. The wrong answers aren't filler either — next to Germany you'll find Austrian towns, Polish clubs and false borders you actually have to know to wave off.

Correct pick: +5, wrong pick: −8. Capped at +100, no floor — a trigger-happy run drops below zero. You get 60 seconds, and it submits itself when time's up. At the end only the choices you ticked light up green or red; the ones you left alone stay dark, so the game never just shows you the full answer.

How to play

Solo. Six puzzles, 30 seconds each, with a 5-second countdown before each one (hit READY to skip it). Two hangman names with a letter or two filled in and an ability as your clue: guess letters with the keys on screen or your keyboard. Then pick a Pokémon's types, call every type that hits it super-effectively, and line up four Pokémon by a base stat.

Each puzzle is worth up to 100. Solving it gets you 30 for sure. The other 70 are for accuracy and speed: every wrong letter or wrong submit cuts what's left by a fifth, and fast solves keep the speed part. Run out of time and it's zero, and the answer stays hidden.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

What we collect

When you use Weeqlash, we collect only what is needed to run the game:

  • Account information — username, email address, and a hashed password (bcrypt). Used for login, email confirmation, password reset, and showing your name to opponents in multiplayer games.
  • Game history — win/loss records, answer accuracy, and match metadata. Stored per account for your stats.
  • Session data — a session identifier stored in Redis, linked to a cookie in your browser. Every visitor gets one; it lasts 30 minutes if you are not logged in and 7 days once you are, and is removed when you sign out.
  • IP address — held in memory for a few minutes to rate-limit login and registration attempts. It is never stored in the database; it appears in server logs only when an address hits the rate limit.

What we do NOT collect

  • Device fingerprints or identifiers beyond the session cookie
  • Behavioural tracking or analytics data
  • Any data from minors knowingly

Data sharing

We do not sell or rent your data. It is passed to a third party only where needed to run the service: our email provider delivers confirmation and password-reset emails to your address, and Google Fonts receives your IP address when your browser loads the site's font. Leaderboard entries are public and show whatever nickname you type in; your username is visible to opponents in multiplayer games — do not use your real name if you prefer anonymity.

Data retention

Account data is kept until you request deletion. You can do that yourself under Stats → Delete my account; once an admin approves, the account is removed together with its stats and game history. Leaderboard entries use a nickname you type in, are not linked to your account, and persist until reset. Session data expires automatically.

Contact

Questions? Ask the site admin directly, or open an issue on the project repository.

Cookie Policy

Last updated: October 2026

Cookies used

Weeqlash uses only the minimum browser storage required to function:

  • Session cookie — a strictly necessary, HTTP-only cookie (connect.sid) set for every visitor so games and logins work. It contains no personal data, just a random session ID, and expires after 7 days at most.
  • localStorage — UI preferences (the last game you opened, whether scores are shown, and your board and duel theme choices) are saved locally in your browser. This data never leaves your device.

No tracking cookies

We do not use advertising cookies, analytics cookies, or any third-party tracking technologies. There are no fingerprinting scripts, pixel trackers, or cross-site identifiers.

Third-party content

The site font is loaded from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com), so your browser sends your IP address to Google when it fetches it. Google handles that data under its privacy policy.

Controlling storage

You can clear localStorage at any time via your browser's developer tools or settings. Doing so will reset your saved preferences.
